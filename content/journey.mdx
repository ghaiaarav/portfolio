## Chapter 1. Patterns Everywhere

I was always drawn to math because it gave structure to phenomena that would otherwise feel disconnected. From scales on the piano to optimal angles in weightlifting, I started noticing underlying patterns waiting to be formalized. I assumed math belonged only in theory or research, so I began college as an engineer looking for tangible applications — but my passion for math never faded.

Competitions and Putnam-style problem sessions showed me how much I enjoy working under a time constraint, where ideas emerge through persistence and necessity. Hosting math club sessions forced me to wrestle with proofs collaboratively, not just alone at a desk.

## Chapter 2. Where Abstraction Meets the Real World

Research pulled the threads together — and humbled me. Estimating galaxy distances with Dr. Romanowsky through stochastic modeling and machine learning exposed how much deeper astrophysics goes than what I currently know. Those moments of uncertainty are the ones that energize me most.

At the SEP Lab, NLP and clustering on learning data became an ACM SIGCSE 2026 paper. RIOT — a classroom observation platform on Google App Engine — is headed to PERC 2026 and arXiv. Knoxville taught me that a 10% reduction in machine downtime beats a clever algorithm nobody deploys.

SCRUGS Lab is where pure math returns, and where I'm proudest right now: combinatorial topology, graph coloring, homology, and 3-manifold arguments toward generalized theorems. LowLa and the F1 Telemetry Explorer are the solo builds — embedding routing for voice, yaw rate for lap time.

## Chapter 3. What's Next

I'm finishing my B.S. in Applied Mathematics (Spring 2028) while seeking environments where abstraction, proof, and modeling are foundations — not side quests. I'm learning Chopin's Ballade No. 1 and planning to perform Liszt's Liebestraum No. 3; performance videos are coming soon.

Between Epictetus, Strogatz, and whatever textbook is open on my desk, I'm usually reading something. Before the labs, there was Hypixel SkyBlock — top ~100 networth built one auction flip at a time, learning market making in a virtual economy with real constraints.

If you're not struggling, you're probably not going deep enough — one commit, one lemma, or one measure at a time.
